### Dream Pools

- Kind, creator, year: 3D art series; Jared Pike; 2019/2020–
- Tags and basis: 1,3,7; a + c: the artist presents the series; IGNANT and Gessato describe it. Tensori credits it as an input for POOLS.
- Status: ongoing
- Theme: calm and uncanny pool spaces with no people. The artist says that empty places can feel like places the viewer has been before.
- Flow: a series of single still images and short loop videos. No narrative. Each image is viewed alone.
- Content outline: pools, arches, stairs, slides, tiled halls, small islands with plants, water channels.
- Art style and rendering: 3D renders made in Blender. Clean, simple geometry and soft, photo-like materials. The series began as practice in rendering water and tile, from a model of a real pool in Italy.
- Lighting and colour: soft daylight from skylights and openings. White and pastel tile, turquoise water, some pink and peach walls. Gentle caustics on walls and floors.
- Camera and media texture: eye-level or slightly low wide views, like architectural photos. Materials: square tile, terrazzo-like floors, clear water with reflection and refraction.
- Space composition and scale: one or two rooms per image, often symmetric. Arches and openings show rooms beyond. Large volumes without furniture or people; scale is mostly human, but some rooms are much taller than normal.
- Sources: https://www.jaredpike.art/projects/dream-pools/ (O); https://www.ignant.com/?p=342312 (M); https://www.gessato.com/dream-pools-artist-jared-pike/ (M); https://aquamagazine.com/builder/pools/article/15123573/dreaming-of-digital-pools (M); https://news.northeastern.edu/?p=270492 (M)
- Images: author Jared Pike; https://www.jaredpike.art/projects/dream-pools/ (author-published images)

### The Backrooms (4chan /x/ thread)

- Kind, creator, year: forum post; anonymous; 2019
- Tags and basis: 2,7; c: Wikipedia + Slashfilm
- Status: archived post (thread started 2019-05-12; caption reply 2019-05-13)
- Theme: a place that feels "off". A user posted a yellow room photo in a thread for disturbing images; a later reply added text that made it a place outside reality.
- Flow: one image and one short caption. Later readers extended it on wikis, videos and games.
- Content outline: the caption describes old wet carpet, one yellow tone, the buzz of fluorescent lights and a near-endless area of empty rooms. A person can arrive there by "noclipping" out of reality.
- Art style and rendering: one real low-quality photo.
- Lighting and colour: yellow walls, beige carpet, flat fluorescent ceiling light.
- Camera and media texture: a slightly blurry digital snapshot with a wide angle and flat flash-like light.
- Space composition and scale: rooms divided by short wall segments, openings without doors, no windows. The caption gives a size of hundreds of millions of square miles; the photo shows one corner.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Backrooms (C); https://slashfilm.com/2182449/the-backrooms-origins-history-explained/ (M)

### The Backrooms (Found Footage)

- Kind, creator, year: video series; Kane Parsons (Kane Pixels); 2022–
- Tags and basis: 2,6,7; a: the author titles it found footage of the Backrooms.
- Status: ongoing (first video 2022-01-07)
- Theme: a research institute in the late 1980s and early 1990s finds and explores the Backrooms. Danger, loss and institutional secrecy.
- Flow: short episodes presented as recovered tapes, out of chronological order. Each is a single camera record or an institute record.
- Content outline: a man noclips into the yellow rooms and is chased; the Async Research Institute builds a machine to reach the space, sends crews and records tests.
- Art style and rendering: 3D animation made in Blender, made to look like real camera footage. Realistic carpet, walls and lights.
- Lighting and colour: yellow walls and fluorescent light; later concrete, dark and red spaces.
- Camera and media texture: 1980s–1990s camcorder look: VHS noise, colour bleeding, tracking errors, date stamps, auto exposure and handheld shake. Tape cuts act as editing.
- Space composition and scale: endless yellow rooms, long corridors, very large dark halls and structures. The camera keeps human eye height, so the size of the space is felt from inside.
- Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H4dGpz6cnHo (O)
- Images: link only (video thumbnail on the author's channel)

### The Oldest View

- Kind, creator, year: video series; Kane Parsons; 2023–2024
- Tags and basis: 6,7; a: the author presents it as found footage.
- Status: ended (six episodes, 2023-03-18 to 2024-09-11)
- Theme: memory and an abandoned mall. Inspired by the real Valley View Center mall in Dallas (demolished 2023).
- Flow: episodes presented as videos by a young vlogger and his friends, in time order.
- Content outline: the vlogger finds stairs in a park that lead to a large mall under the ground. A silent giant figure is in the mall.
- Art style and rendering: 3D animation made to look like real camera footage.
- Lighting and colour: dark interiors lit by camera light; old mall colours.
- Camera and media texture: consumer camera and vlog style, with handheld motion. Exact device not shown in sources.
- Space composition and scale: a very large underground mall with many floors, atriums and long halls. The giant figure gives the scale.
- Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wWNMsZ44ooc (O); https://www.slashfilm.com/1458471/the-oldest-view-scariest-movie-of-2023-youtube (M); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oldest_View (C)
