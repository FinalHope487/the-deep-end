### Dreamcore (demo "Dreampools")

- Kind, creator, year: game; Montraluz; 2025
- Tags and basis: 1,2,3; a: the game is named Dreamcore; the studio names liminal spaces, the Backrooms and dreamlike architecture as subjects. Dreampools is one named area.
- Status: released (2025-01-23; more maps added after launch)
- Theme: familiar places become disorienting when layout, scale and function change. Exploration depends on memory, observation and sense of direction.
- Flow: large maps with no fixed route and little guidance. Each map has its own layout.
- Content outline: Dreampools: tiled pools, corridors, ramps, stairs. Eternal Suburbia: repeating houses in a strange neighbourhood. The official site also lists Playrooms and Liminal Hotel.
- Art style and rendering: detailed 3D of ordinary architecture in unusual arrangements: water, ceramic tile, suburban houses, play structures.
- Lighting and colour: Dreampools uses pale tile and soft light. Eternal Suburbia has a day-night cycle.
- Camera and media texture: first person. Water surfaces and repeated materials. Lens or VHS filter not shown in sources.
- Space composition and scale: Dreampools links open rooms, dark corners and passages with no fixed direction. Eternal Suburbia repeats house exteriors but varies interiors. Large spaces and returning routes make orientation hard.
- Sources: https://store.steampowered.com/app/2453060/ (O); https://www.dreamcoregame.com/ (O)

### Dreamcore: Rabbit Hole

- Kind, creator, year: game; Ankoku; 2026
- Tags and basis: 2,3,5; a: the studio names dreamcore and liminal spaces as inspirations and describes a softer, less frightening reading of them.
- Status: released (2026-04-24; Highway and Dreampools added 2026-08)
- Theme: nostalgic places recall an idealised past. Comfort with mild uncertainty.
- Flow: Story Mode: a person looks for a way back to reality. Relax Mode: free exploration with no goals.
- Content outline: several dream worlds with their own architecture and sound. Highway: a coastal holiday road and motel. Dreampools: a warmer pool area.
- Art style and rendering: realistic-looking spaces that mix dream scenery with familiar buildings.
- Lighting and colour: one atmosphere per location; soft and dreamlike, not constant darkness.
- Camera and media texture: first person; field-of-view setting. VHS and lens distortion not shown in sources.
- Space composition and scale: separate worlds with buildings, outdoor areas and connected spaces. Routes support directed exploration and free wandering.
- Sources: https://store.steampowered.com/app/4149700/ (O); https://steamcommunity.com/app/4149700/allnews/ (O)

### Millennium Dream / 千禧夢

- Kind, creator, year: game; LucidDreamLab; 2026
- Tags and basis: 3,5; a: the creator calls it Chinese dreamcore (中式夢核). Chinese media confirm the nostalgic walking-and-photography design.
- Status: released (2026-01-28 in Chinese reports; Steam lists 2026-01-27)
- Theme: everyday objects and places from childhood recall life around the year 2000. Nostalgia as comfort and as knowledge that the past is gone.
- Flow: walk through remembered places, take photos, examine objects. Observation, not a mission sequence.
- Content outline: a childhood home, a pedestrian street, an empty school. Familiar objects, a few conversations and details give fragments of memory.
- Art style and rendering: Unreal Engine 5. Detailed Chinese urban and domestic interiors; period objects, signs and furniture.
- Lighting and colour: warm afternoon sun. The player can change weather and time of day: rain, snow, dusk, morning.
- Camera and media texture: in-game camera with filters, including an old mobile-phone grain effect and high-contrast black and white.
- Space composition and scale: homes, streets and schools without crowds. Objects and room layout carry the meaning.
- Sources: https://store.steampowered.com/app/3470560/ (O); https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_32948255 (M); https://www.gamersky.com/news/202601/2081928.shtml (M)
- Images: steam-3470560-1.jpg; steam-3470560-2.jpg

Note: The Paper reads the game as environmental storytelling where spaces and objects are the subject. This is critic analysis, not a stated creator intent.

### Yume Nikki

- Kind, creator, year: game; Kikiyama; 2004
- Tags and basis: 4; c: a Press Start study names unusual maps, movement and wandering as central; WIRED describes exploration of connected dream places. A dreamcore label is not proven.
- Status: released (free distribution 2004; Steam 2018)
- Theme: dreams as spaces for isolated, open-ended exploration. Places have no explained meaning.
- Flow: Madotsuki enters dreams from her bedroom. Connected worlds; the player collects effects and returns to earlier places.
- Content outline: forests, strange rooms, outdoor areas and surreal figures. Events appear without a quest sequence.
- Art style and rendering: RPG Maker 2D pixel art; known objects mixed with abstract images.
- Lighting and colour: one palette per area with strong contrast; some dark, some bright or unusual.
- Camera and media texture: top-down 2D. No lens or VHS treatment.
- Space composition and scale: rooms and landscapes that look disconnected form one dream network. Some areas loop at their edges. Odd spatial relations push wandering over route planning.
- Sources: https://press-start.gla.ac.uk/press-start/article/view/226 (R); https://www.wired.com/story/yume-nikki-japanese-game-that-took-14-years-to-officially-come-out/ (M); https://store.steampowered.com/app/650700/ (O)
- Images: steam-650700-1.jpg; steam-650700-2.jpg

### LSD: Dream Emulator

- Kind, creator, year: game; Asmik Ace; 1998
- Tags and basis: 4; c: the game-studies sources describe first-person exploration of changing dream places. A dreamcore label is not established.
- Status: released (1998, PlayStation, Japan)
- Theme: dreams shown through changing spaces, not a plot.
- Flow: dream days. Each session is a short timed walk; touching some objects moves the player to another place.
- Content outline: a village, a port, a shrine and other surreal areas. Movement and observation, no missions.
- Art style and rendering: early PlayStation 3D with unusual textures and objects.
- Lighting and colour: different colours and patterns per location. Method not shown in sources.
- Camera and media texture: first person, low-resolution PlayStation rendering.
- Space composition and scale: locations connect by contact transitions, not corridors. Repeat visits give different paths.
- Sources: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/publication/isbn/9783839443040/downloadAsset/9783839443040_Leseprobe.pdf (R); https://criticalvideogamestudies.com/game-review-the-surrealism-of-lsd-dream-emulator/ (R)

### Hypnospace Outlaw

- Kind, creator, year: game; Tendershoot; 2019
- Tags and basis: 3 (early-web visual aesthetic only); c: PC Gamer and GameSpot describe a detailed recreation of late-1990s personal websites and internet culture.
- Status: released (2019-03-12)
- Theme: an imagined 1999 internet: personal creativity, online communities, corporate control.
- Flow: the player is an online content moderator. Read sites, search for clues, find hidden pages, report rule violations.
- Content outline: personal sites, themed communities, adverts, files and software in the fictional Hypnospace network. Pages change as the story goes on.
- Art style and rendering: pixel graphics, crowded layouts, animated GIFs, decorative fonts, tiled backgrounds.
- Lighting and colour: bright interface colours, patterned backgrounds, contrasting text. No 3D lighting.
- Camera and media texture: a simulated desktop and browser. Low-resolution graphics, web animations, custom cursors, looping MIDI audio.
- Space composition and scale: a network of websites, not rooms. Themed zones, links, hidden pages and search results are the routes.
- Sources: https://www.pcgamer.com/hypnospace-outlaw-review/ (M); https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/hypnospace-outlaw-review-weird-world-web/1900-6417106/ (M); https://store.steampowered.com/app/844590/ (O)
- Images: steam-844590-1.jpg; steam-844590-2.jpg

### CARI (Y2K Aesthetic Institute) archive

- Kind, creator, year: archive; Evan Collins and collaborators; 2013–
- Tags and basis: 3,7; a: the project's history documents research and archiving of Y2K and related aesthetics. Collins began collecting in 2013; the groups and archive came later.
- Status: ongoing
- Theme: consumer design aesthetics of the recent past, identified, documented and compared through original examples.
- Flow: browse research collections, design categories and historical material. Reference, not a story.
- Content outline: Y2K and later consumer aesthetics; early image collections, community archives, presentations, research.
- Art style and rendering: graphic design, advertising, industrial design, architecture, fashion and digital media. No single style.
- Lighting and colour: per aesthetic; the history names palettes and design features for specific styles.
- Camera and media texture: images from many sources; the research notes lost images and obsolete formats.
- Space composition and scale: physical space is not the main structure. Some collections show architecture, shop interiors and product settings.
- Sources: https://cari.institute/history (O); https://cari.institute/ (O)
- Images: Evan Collins and collaborators; https://cari.institute/history

Note: CARI is a research archive, not one artwork. It is here because its creators document and classify the aesthetics of category 3.
